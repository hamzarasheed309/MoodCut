Composition rules

  • Anchor: one primary subject or diagram; keep it distinct from the headline.
  • Read order: hook → human / object → evidence → source.
  • Scale: title should survive a small phone preview; metadata may not.
  • Air: leave 7% horizontal safe space and 10% vertical for player UI.
  • Texture: use halftone, paper, grid or map lines at low opacity—never all at once.
  • Split-screen: reserve for explicit comparisons; keep the seam meaningful.
  • Maps: simplify to the claim. Mark geography accurately; label disputed boundaries neutrally.
  • Data: label units and baseline. Animate the comparison, not decoration.
04 / Presenter anchor

Keep Hamza in the scene.

When your voice continues over B-roll, the audience should still feel your presence. Treat the camera as a designed layer—not a webcam box pasted on at the end.

THE STORY PLAYS.

01 · Glass picture-in-pictureDEFAULT FOR B-ROLL

Place a live 4:3 or 16:9 crop in a translucent rounded glass frame, typically 18–24% of width. Add a small LIVE label; no thick device bezel.

02 · Floating cut-outUSE FOR GRAPHIC BEATS

Mask the presenter into a soft organic cut-out at an edge of the frame. Keep shoulders/hands, add a delicate mint rim-light, and never cover the key text.

03 · Full-frame hostUSE FOR IMPORTANT LINES

Return to a full camera shot for a personal claim, emotional beat, new question or concluding thought. Let the frame breathe before graphics resume.

Picture-in-picture rules: the face window remains live while the main image changes; choose a corner with negative space and move it only between beats. Keep eyes in the upper third, preserve headroom, use consistent warm skin tone and soft key light. If on-screen text or a chart needs the corner, move the PiP to the opposite safe corner with a short, calm 6–8 frame slide. Don’t shrink the face below 16% of frame width for long stretches, freeze-frame it, or let a glass reflection cross the eyes.

07 / Transitions

The default is a hard cut. Branded transitions punctuate a chapter change, a shift in scale or a reveal.

09 / Sound design

Small cues. Strong point of view.

Sound supports clarity and emotional rhythm. Dialogue stays intelligible and in front of every designed accent.

Palette

  • Bed: restrained percussive pulse, warm analog keys, muted bass, or location texture.
  • Evidence: dry paper tick, soft click, restrained bell or low thump—one cue per key reveal.
  • Motion: short whoosh only when a graphic travels a meaningful distance.
  • Pakistan texture: use recorded ambience, instruments and voices with context and care; avoid generic “oriental” loops.

Mix guardrails

  • Dialogue is the anchor; duck beds 6–10 dB beneath speech as a starting point.
  • Keep peaks safe; leave headroom for the platform encode. No clipped sfx or startling full-scale hits.
  • Use silence before a difficult statistic or after a human quote when it improves comprehension.
  • Music changes should mark a chapter or emotional turn; avoid a new cue every 20 seconds.

Cutting principles

DOTrim filler, preserve thought. Use J/L cuts when the next idea should arrive early. Let key figures sit on screen long enough to read.
DON’TSpeed-ramp talking heads by default, stack punch-ins on every sentence, or cover every pause with b-roll.
  • Use a motivated crop to shift attention, not to manufacture energy.
  • Keep eye line, gaze and screen direction consistent across a comparison.
  • Subtitles: sentence case, 1–2 lines, max ~38 characters per line, safe-area aware.
  • For vertical derivatives, recompose type and diagrams; do not just crop the 16:9 master.
11 / Story recipes

Reusable starting points—not formulas.

The topic is still undecided. Choose a structure only after the episode idea is known; these are flexible examples.

EXPLAINER · 6–12 MIN

The mechanism

Use when an audience needs to understand how a system works.

  1. Open on a person’s question.
  2. Map the system in three steps.
  3. Show who sets the rules and who bears the cost.
  4. Test the explanation against one sourced example.
  5. Close with what would change the outcome.
SECTOR DEEP DIVE

The ledger

Use for industries, incentives, market structure and trade-offs.

  1. Lead with a tension, not the sector name.
  2. Set the market boundary and time period.
  3. Trace money, ownership or supply.
  4. Compare the stated promise to observed evidence.
  5. State uncertainty and the open question.
SHORT · 30–90 SEC

The one-number story

Use when one counterintuitive measure unlocks a bigger idea.

  1. Show the number and its unit immediately.
  2. Ask what it leaves out.
  3. Visualize one comparison or scale shift.
  4. Add the source in-frame.
  5. End on the implication, not “like and subscribe.”
12 / AI source of truth

Paste this into the editing brief.

Channel identity is stable; topic, format and tone can vary by episode. Add those specifics only once decided.

You are editing a video for “The Moodbook of Hamza.” Follow this visual and editorial system as the source of truth.

CHANNEL DNA
Make complex systems in Pakistan understandable, human and evidence-led. The tone is curious, clear-eyed, conversational and respectful. Show the mechanism behind a headline. Do not talk down to viewers, manufacture outrage, or flatten people into examples.

VISUAL IDENTITY
Editorial newsroom × clear SaaS interface. Use warm Archive Paper (#F4F2EB) and Newsroom Ink (#171B19) as the base. Signal Green (#12A982) marks the key insight; Alert Pink (#ED2860) marks friction or human cost; Evidence Yellow (#F7C845) highlights decisive numbers. Ledger Blue (#3378EF) is reserved for institutions, infrastructure or data. Use one lead signal color and at most one support color in a shot.

TYPE
Display: Archivo Black (fallback Arial Black), 900 weight, tight tracking and 0.88–0.96 leading. Thumbnail accent: Barlow Condensed ExtraBold (fallback Impact), only for short phrases. Body/subtitles: Inter or Segoe UI, high contrast, sentence case. Data/source labels: IBM Plex Mono or Consolas. Urdu: Noto Nastaliq Urdu for short phrases; preserve correct script shaping and have Urdu reviewed by a fluent reader. Never put critical small text in a decorative face.

COMPOSITION
Master 1920×1080, 16:9. Keep key titles inside 7% side and 10% top/bottom safe margins. One dominant idea per frame, with read order: hook → person/place → evidence → source. Ensure title readability at phone-preview size. Pair abstract systems with a human-scale object, person or place. Label units, baselines, time ranges and sources. Maps must be accurate and neutral. Recompose for vertical; do not simply crop.

MOTION
Motion explains a relationship: trace routes and money, reveal a comparison with a clean wipe, or pop one decisive statistic and let it settle. Use confident ease-out motion; typical entry 380–650 ms, data reveals 700–1400 ms, micro-cues 120–220 ms. Hold key data 2.5–5 seconds or as long as needed to read. Use hard cuts by default. Branded transitions are reserved for a real shift in chapter, scale or evidence. Avoid constant movement, random glitch, elastic bounce, excessive zooms and transition-on-every-cut.

GRAPHICS & EFFECTS
Lower thirds identify, explain or prove: kicker → concise title → context/source. Keep them within title-safe, usually no more than two text lines; animate in 7–10 frames at 30 fps and leave long enough to read. Use subtle halftone, paper grain, photocopy edge, blueprint/map lines only when they relate to the story. Texture is a quiet material layer, never a readability problem. Avoid bevels, heavy glow, permanent logo bugs, fake VHS, generic “tech HUD,” excessive RGB split or fake archival damage.

EDIT & SOUND
Cut on thought, action or change of evidence. Pace follows idea density and stakes. Preserve the full thought; do not punch-in or speed-ramp by default. Dialogue leads the mix; duck music under speech, use restrained cues, and leave silence when it helps a difficult fact land. Use recorded Pakistani ambience/music with context and care; avoid generic cultural shorthand. Subtitles are sentence case, 1–2 lines, high contrast and safe-area aware.

EDITORIAL SAFETY
Do not invent facts, sources, quotes, statistics, maps or certainty. Keep provenance on-screen for consequential claims. Distinguish fact, estimate, allegation and opinion. Preserve caveats and date ranges. If information is missing, flag it for the producer instead of filling the gap. Protect dignity and context when showing affected people.

FOR THIS EPISODE, ASK FOR
Topic and thesis · target audience · runtime and aspect ratio · script or outline · source links and claim-to-source notes · available footage/stills/charts · Urdu/English preference · CTA (if any) · any claims needing legal or editorial review.

OUTPUT
Propose a story-first edit plan with timecodes, narration, picture, graphics, motion, sound, source labels and any missing evidence. Keep one dominant visual idea per beat. Make the result unmistakably The Moodbook of Hamza: bold like an editorial thumbnail, legible like a good product interface, grounded like a reported story.
